Here is the updated version with **more detailed headings, columns, and table-format instructions**, especially for **FIFO, LIFO, weighted average, moving average, and stock cards**.

# Exam Approach: Step-by-Step Formatting Guide

Before answering any practical accounting question, first identify the type of task. Decide whether the question is asking for a journal entry, T-account, bank reconciliation, cash flow statement, inventory schedule, stock card, depreciation calculation, liability journal entry, ratio calculation, or theory answer. Once you know the task, set up the correct format before doing calculations.

For every practical question, use this order: write the heading or account name first, prepare the main answer in the correct format, show workings underneath, and clearly label the final figure. Do not begin with random calculations unless the question only asks for a calculation. In accounting, marks are often awarded for structure, not just the final number.

---

# 1. General Table Formatting Rules

For any table or schedule, always use a clear heading first. The heading should tell the marker three things: the business name if given, the type of answer, and the period or date.

For a statement, use:

Business Name
Statement Name
for the year ended [date]

For a point-in-time statement, use:

Business Name
Statement Name
as at [date]

For an inventory schedule, use:

Business Name
Inventory Costing Schedule
[Method Name]
[Periodic System or Perpetual System]
for the period ended [date]

For a stock card, use:

Business Name
Inventory Stock Card
[Method Name]
Perpetual System
for the period ended [date]

Keep column headings clear and consistent. Amount columns should be aligned on the right. Use the same terms throughout the answer. For example, do not switch between **Cost of Goods Sold** and **Cost of Sales** unless the question does.

---

# 2. Bank Reconciliation Question

Start with a three-line heading. The first line is the business name. The second line is **Bank Reconciliation Statement**. The third line is **as at [date]**. Use **as at** because a bank reconciliation is prepared for one specific date.

Begin with **Balance as per bank statement**. Copy the balance exactly from the question and clearly label it as **Dr** or **Cr**. A bank statement **Cr** means the bank shows money available. A bank statement **Dr** means the bank shows an overdraft.

Use the exemplar method. If the bank statement starts with a **Dr overdraft**, add unpresented cheques because they make the overdraft worse, and deduct deposits not credited because they reduce the overdraft. If the bank statement starts with a **Cr balance**, add deposits not credited because they increase the bank balance, and deduct unpresented cheques because they reduce the bank balance.

List every cheque separately if cheque numbers are given. Write the cheque number first, then the amount. After listing all cheques, show the total. This matches the exemplar format and gives method marks.

Use this structure:

Business Name
Bank Reconciliation Statement
as at [date]

Balance as per bank statement Dr/Cr

Add: [item]
Cheque No. [number]
Cheque No. [number]
Total add items

Less: [item]
Total less items

Add/Less: Bank error

Cash at Bank balance Dr/Cr

Finish with **Balance as per Cash at Bank account** or **Cash at Bank balance**. Label the final balance as **Dr** or **Cr**.

---

# 3. Direct Cash Flow Statement Question

Start with a three-line heading. The first line is the business name. The second line is **Statement of Cash Flows**. The third line is **for the year ended [date]**. Use **for the year ended** because a cash flow statement covers a period.

Use three sections in this order:

Cash flows from operating activities
Cash flows from investing activities
Cash flows from financing activities

For operating activities, use the direct method. Start with **Cash receipts from customers**. Then list cash payments such as **Cash paid to suppliers and employees**, wages paid, rent paid, tax paid, or other operating payments.

Cash inflows are shown as positive amounts. Cash outflows are shown in brackets. Do not use Dr or Cr in a cash flow statement.

Use this structure:

Business Name
Statement of Cash Flows
for the year ended [date]

Cash flows from operating activities
Cash receipts from customers
Cash paid to suppliers and employees
Net cash from operating activities

Cash flows from investing activities
Purchase of equipment
Proceeds from sale of equipment
Net cash used in/from investing activities

Cash flows from financing activities
Loan received
Drawings/dividends paid
Net cash used in/from financing activities

Net increase/decrease in cash
Cash at beginning of period
Cash at end of period

If the question asks only for an extract, prepare only the required section but still use the correct heading and subtotal.

# 4. Direct Cash Flow Workings

For cash receipts from customers, use this working format:

Cash receipts from customers
Sales revenue
Add: Beginning accounts receivable
Less: Ending accounts receivable
Cash receipts from customers

For cash paid to suppliers, use this working format:

Cash paid to suppliers
Cost of sales
Less: Beginning inventory
Add: Ending inventory
Purchases
Add: Beginning accounts payable
Less: Ending accounts payable
Cash paid to suppliers

For cash paid for expenses, use this working format:

Cash paid for expenses
Expenses
Less: Depreciation or other non-cash expenses
Add: Beginning expense payable
Less: Ending expense payable
Cash paid for expenses

Always show workings underneath the cash flow statement. Do not hide calculations inside the statement.

# 5. Receivables and Bad Debts Question

Start by identifying the method. If the question gives a percentage of credit sales or net credit sales, that percentage gives the current year **Bad Debts Expense**. Do not subtract the existing allowance balance. If the question gives an ageing schedule of accounts receivable, the estimate gives the required ending balance in **Allowance for Doubtful Debts**, so compare it with the existing allowance and record only the adjustment.

For journal entries, use four columns:

Date
Account
Debit
Credit

Write the debit account first and the credit account underneath.

For a write-off under the allowance method:

Dr Allowance for Doubtful Debts
Cr Accounts Receivable Control / debtor

For a recovery using the mark scheme style:

Dr Accounts Receivable
Cr Bad Debts Recovered

Then:

Dr Cash at Bank
Cr Accounts Receivable

For a year-end adjustment under the percentage of credit sales method:

Dr Bad Debts Expense
Cr Allowance for Doubtful Debts

If asked for the Allowance for Doubtful Debts account, prepare a T-account. Do not only show a top-up schedule.

---

# 6. Allowance for Doubtful Debts T-Account Format

Write the account heading as:

Allowance for Doubtful Debts

Separate the account into a debit side and a credit side.

Debit side includes decreases in the allowance, usually write-offs. Credit side includes increases in the allowance, usually opening balance and bad debts expense.

Use this layout:

Allowance for Doubtful Debts

Debit side:
Date — Detail — Amount
Date — Balance c/d — Amount
Total

Credit side:
Date — Balance b/d — Amount
Date — Bad Debts Expense — Amount
Total

Balance b/d next period: Amount Cr

Balance c/d closes the account. Balance b/d opens the next period. Since Allowance for Doubtful Debts is normally a credit balance, Balance c/d usually appears on the debit side and Balance b/d appears on the credit side.

# 7. Inventory Question: First Formatting Decision

For every inventory question, first identify whether the system is **periodic** or **perpetual**.

If it is **periodic**, prepare an **Inventory Costing Schedule**. Do not prepare a stock card. The periodic system calculates ending inventory and cost of goods sold at the end of the period.

If it is **perpetual**, prepare an **Inventory Stock Card**. The perpetual system updates inventory after every purchase and every sale.

Then identify the method: FIFO, LIFO, specific identification, weighted average, or moving average.

Use the correct heading:

For periodic:

Business Name
Inventory Costing Schedule
[Method Name]
Periodic System
for the period ended [date]

For perpetual:

Business Name
Inventory Stock Card
[Method Name]
Perpetual System
for the period ended [date]

---

# 8. Periodic Inventory Schedule: Main Table Format

For a periodic inventory schedule, use three sections.

First section: **Goods available for sale**.

Use these columns:

Date
Explanation
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

Format:

Goods available for sale

Date — Explanation — Units — Unit Cost — Total Cost
[date] — Beginning inventory — units — cost — total
[date] — Purchase — units — cost — total
[date] — Purchase — units — cost — total
Total goods available for sale — total units — total cost

Second section: **Ending inventory**.

Use these columns:

Date/source
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

Third section: **Cost of goods sold**.

Use this format:

Goods available for sale
Less: Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold

This is the correct periodic format for FIFO, LIFO, specific identification, and weighted average.

---

# 9. FIFO Periodic Table Format

FIFO means first in, first out. The earliest units are assumed sold first, so ending inventory is made up of the most recent purchases.

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Costing Schedule
FIFO Method
Periodic System
for the period ended [date]

Section 1: Goods available for sale

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

List beginning inventory first, then each purchase in date order.

Section 2: Ending inventory

Columns:

Date/source
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

For FIFO periodic, fill this section using the newest layers first. Start with the latest purchase. If ending inventory needs more units, use the next most recent purchase layer.

Section 3: Cost of goods sold

Goods available for sale
Less: Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold

The key formatting point is that the ending inventory table must clearly show the latest purchase layers.

---

# 10. LIFO Periodic Table Format

Heading:

LIFO Method
Periodic System
for the period ended [date]

Section 1: Goods available for sale

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

List beginning inventory first, then purchases in date order.

Section 2: Ending inventory

Columns:

Date/source
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

For LIFO periodic, fill this section using the oldest layers first. Start with beginning inventory. If ending inventory needs more units, use the earliest purchase layer.

Section 3: Cost of goods sold

Goods available for sale
Less: Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold

The key formatting point is that the ending inventory table must clearly show the oldest layers.

# 11. Specific Identification Table Format

Specific identification uses the actual units identified in the question. Do not assume FIFO or LIFO.

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Costing Schedule
Specific Identification Method
Periodic System
for the period ended [date]

Section 1: Goods available for sale

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

Section 2: Ending inventory or cost of goods sold

Use the exact units identified in the question.

Columns:

Identified source
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

If the question identifies units on hand, calculate ending inventory first. Then calculate cost of goods sold.

If the question identifies units sold, calculate cost of goods sold first. Then calculate ending inventory.

Section 3: Final summary

Goods available for sale
Less: Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold

The key formatting point is that the source of the units must be clear.

---

# 12. Weighted Average Periodic Table Format

Weighted average belongs to the periodic system.

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Costing Schedule
Weighted Average Method
Periodic System
for the period ended [date]

Section 1: Goods available for sale

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

List beginning inventory and all purchases. Total the units and total cost.

Section 2: Weighted average cost

Use this format:

Total cost of goods available for sale
Total units available for sale
Weighted average cost per unit

Then calculate:

Weighted average cost per unit = total cost ÷ total units

Section 3: Ending inventory and cost of goods sold

Use these lines:

Ending inventory units × weighted average cost
Cost of goods sold units × weighted average cost

Or:

Goods available for sale
Less: Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold

The key formatting point is that the weighted average cost calculation must appear before ending inventory and cost of goods sold.

# 13. Perpetual Stock Card: Main Heading and Columns

For perpetual inventory, prepare a stock card.

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Stock Card
[Method Name]
Perpetual System
for the period ended [date]

The stock card columns must be:

Date
Explanation
Purchases
Cost of Sales
Balance

Under **Purchases**, use three sub-columns:

Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

Under **Cost of Sales**, use three sub-columns:

Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

Under **Balance**, use three sub-columns:

Units
Unit Cost
Total Cost

Full stock card heading:

Date | Explanation | Purchases: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost | Cost of Sales: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost | Balance: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost

In a handwritten exam, abbreviate like this:

Date | Explanation | Purchases | Cost of Sales | Balance
Units | Unit Cost | Total Cost under each money section

Start with beginning inventory in the Balance column only. Purchases go in the Purchases column. Sales go in the Cost of Sales column. After every transaction, update the Balance column.

Do not include selling price unless the question specifically asks for it. The stock card records inventory at cost.

# 14. FIFO Perpetual Stock Card Format

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Stock Card
FIFO Method
Perpetual System
for the period ended [date]

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Purchases: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost
Cost of Sales: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost
Balance: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost

How to fill it:

Opening inventory goes in Balance only.

For purchases, record the units, unit cost, and total cost in Purchases. Then add that layer to Balance.

For sales, remove the oldest units first. Record the cost of the units sold in Cost of Sales. Then update Balance.

If the sale uses units from more than one layer, split the sale across separate lines in the Cost of Sales section.

In the Balance column, keep remaining layers separate.

At the end:

Total the Cost of Sales column = Cost of Goods Sold.
Final Balance column = Ending Inventory.

The key formatting point is that FIFO perpetual must show inventory layers in the Balance column.

---

# 15. LIFO Perpetual Stock Card Format

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Stock Card
LIFO Method
Perpetual System
for the period ended [date]

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Purchases: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost
Cost of Sales: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost
Balance: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost

How to fill it:

Opening inventory goes in Balance only.

For purchases, record the purchase and add it as a new layer in Balance.

For sales, remove the newest units first. Record the cost of those units in Cost of Sales. Then update Balance.

If the sale uses more than one layer, split the Cost of Sales over separate lines.

In the Balance column, show the remaining layers separately.

At the end:

Total Cost of Sales = Cost of Goods Sold.
Final Balance = Ending Inventory.

The key formatting point is that LIFO perpetual removes the newest layer first at each sale date, not just at the end of the period.

---

# 16. Moving Average Stock Card Format

Moving average belongs to the perpetual system.

Heading:

Business Name
Inventory Stock Card
Moving Average Method
Perpetual System
for the period ended [date]

Columns:

Date
Explanation
Purchases: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost
Cost of Sales: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost
Balance: Units, Unit Cost, Total Cost

How to fill it:

Opening inventory goes in Balance only.

For a purchase, record the purchase in the Purchases column. Then calculate a new average cost in the Balance column.

New average cost = total balance cost ÷ total balance units.

For a sale, use the current average cost. Record the sale in the Cost of Sales column using that average cost. Then reduce the Balance units and total cost.

Do not calculate a new average after a sale. Only recalculate the average after a purchase.

Unlike FIFO and LIFO, the Balance column normally has one combined average-cost line, not separate cost layers.

At the end:

Total Cost of Sales = Cost of Goods Sold.
Final Balance = Ending Inventory.

The key formatting point is that moving average must show a new average cost after each purchase.

# 17. Inventory Final Summary Format

After every inventory schedule or stock card, finish with a final summary.

For periodic inventory, write:

Cost of goods available for sale
Less: Ending inventory
Cost of goods sold

For perpetual stock cards, write:

Cost of goods sold
Ending inventory

If the question asks for both cost of goods sold and ending inventory, label both clearly. Do not leave the marker to find them inside the table.

---

# 18. Lower of Cost and Net Realisable Value

After calculating inventory cost, compare cost with net realisable value.

Use this format:

Inventory at cost
Net realisable value
Inventory should be valued at lower amount

If net realisable value is lower, state that inventory is written down to NRV. If cost is lower, state that inventory remains at cost.

This rule applies only to inventory.

---

# 19. Non-Current Asset Question

Start by identifying the event: acquisition, depreciation, overhaul, disposal, scrapping, revaluation, amortisation, or impairment.

If the asset is purchased, record the asset at cost. If GST applies, separate GST outlay. Credit Cash at Bank if paid immediately, or Accounts Payable if bought on credit.

If depreciation is required, identify the method first. Then write the formula, substitute the numbers, calculate depreciation, and record the journal entry. The entry is debit Depreciation Expense and credit Accumulated Depreciation.

For straight-line depreciation, calculate cost minus residual value, then divide by useful life.

For diminishing balance, multiply the opening carrying amount by the depreciation rate.

For units-of-production, calculate depreciation per unit, then multiply by units used.

For sum-of-years-digits, calculate the sum of the years, then apply the correct fraction for the year.

For disposal, update depreciation to the disposal date first. Then remove the asset cost, remove accumulated depreciation, record cash received, and record the gain or loss.

---

# 20. Liabilities Question

Start by identifying the type of liability.

For warranties, calculate the estimated warranty obligation. Record debit Warranty Expense and credit Provision for Warranties.

For bills payable, write the principal first. Then calculate interest using principal × rate × time. At maturity, debit Bills Payable, debit Interest Expense or Interest Payable, and credit Cash at Bank.

For debentures, follow the sequence in the exemplar. When application money is received, debit Cash Trust and credit Application—Debentures. When cash is transferred, debit Cash at Bank and credit Cash Trust. When debentures are allotted, debit Application—Debentures and credit Debentures. When interest is paid, debit Debenture Interest Expense and credit Cash at Bank. When interest expense is closed, debit Profit and Loss Summary and credit Debenture Interest Expense.

For employee benefits, debit the wage, salary, annual leave, or long service leave expense. Credit each payable account separately.

For contingent liabilities, do not force a journal entry. If the obligation is uncertain but material, write a note disclosure answer.

---

# 21. Ratio Question

Start by writing the ratio name. Then write the formula exactly as given. Then substitute the numbers. Then calculate the answer. Then interpret the result.

The interpretation should say what the ratio measures, whether a higher or lower number is better, and what the ratio should be compared against.

A good ratio answer order is:

Ratio name
Formula
Substitution
Answer
Interpretation

Do not only give the number.

---

# 22. Theory Question

Read the command word first. If it says define, give a definition. If it says explain, give the definition and the reason. If it says distinguish, compare the two terms. If it says discuss, give more than one developed point.

Use short paragraphs or dot points. Each point should be a full idea.

For Conceptual Framework theory, use precise terms such as relevance, faithful representation, comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability, asset, liability, equity, income, expense, recognition, and measurement.

For theory connected to practical accounting, explain the purpose. For example, bank reconciliation is an internal control because it compares business records with the bank’s independent records.

---

# 23. General Presentation Strategy

Start each question on a new page or clearly separated section.

Use headings. Use the exact account names from the question where possible.

For journal entries, use Date, Account, Debit, and Credit.

For T-accounts, show debit side, credit side, Balance c/d, and Balance b/d where needed.

For periodic inventory, use an inventory costing schedule.

For perpetual inventory, use a stock card.

For stock cards, update the Balance column after every transaction.

For statements, use the correct heading and date wording.

Use **as at** for bank reconciliation.

Use **for the year ended** for statement of cash flows.

Use brackets for cash outflows and negative amounts.

Use Dr and Cr for journals, ledgers, and bank reconciliation balances.

Do not use Dr and Cr in cash flow statements, inventory schedules, depreciation schedules, or ratio calculations.

Always show workings. Even if the final number is wrong, clear workings can still earn method marks.
